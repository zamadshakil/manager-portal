# Operations Team — Document Formatting Audit Report

**Date:** May 11, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Operations Team  
**Review Period:** Q1–Q2 2026  
**Classification:** Internal

---

## 1. Overview

This report presents the findings of an internal formatting audit conducted across all operational documents submitted during Q1 and Q2 of 2026. The audit was initiated following feedback from senior management that inconsistent document structure was making review cycles longer than necessary.

### 1.1 Audit Scope

- Total documents reviewed: 34
- Document types included: weekly updates, monthly reports, proposals, and briefing notes
- Reviewed by: two senior analysts using a standardised scoring rubric

### 1.2 Audit Methodology

Each document was scored on four dimensions:
1. Presence of clearly labelled sections
2. Consistent heading hierarchy (H1 → H2 → H3)
3. Use of structured lists rather than paragraph-embedded items
4. Adequate separation between sections and paragraphs

---

## 2. Key Findings

### 2.1 Compliance by Document Type

| Document Type | Total Reviewed | Fully Compliant | Partially Compliant | Non-Compliant |
|--------------|----------------|-----------------|---------------------|---------------|
| Weekly Updates | 12 | 9 (75%) | 2 (17%) | 1 (8%) |
| Monthly Reports | 10 | 6 (60%) | 3 (30%) | 1 (10%) |
| Proposals | 8 | 7 (88%) | 1 (12%) | 0 (0%) |
| Briefing Notes | 4 | 2 (50%) | 1 (25%) | 1 (25%) |

### 2.2 Most Common Violations

1. **Missing section headings** — 11 of 34 documents had one or more sections with no heading, requiring readers to infer structure from context.
2. **Embedded list items** — 8 documents presented multiple items as comma-separated clauses inside paragraphs rather than as bullet or numbered lists.
3. **Inconsistent heading levels** — 5 documents mixed heading styles (e.g., bold inline text treated as a heading alongside properly formatted headings).
4. **No document header** — 4 documents had no title, date, or author information at the top.

### 2.3 Positive Observations

- Proposal documents had the highest compliance rate (88%), likely due to the established proposal template.
- All 34 documents used professional language and contained no inappropriate content.
- Average document length was appropriate for the document type in 91% of cases.

---

## 3. Recommendations

1. **Mandate the use of section templates for weekly updates and briefing notes.** Pre-formatted templates have already proven effective for proposals and should be extended to all recurring document types.

2. **Conduct a 30-minute formatting workshop for the full team** before the start of Q3. Focus areas: heading hierarchy, list formatting, and document header requirements.

3. **Add a formatting checklist to the submission process.** A simple five-point checklist attached to the submission form will prompt authors to verify structure before uploading.

4. **Assign peer-review pairs for monthly reports.** Each analyst pairs with a colleague to review formatting before the report is submitted to management. This adds minimal time but significantly improves output quality.

---

## 4. Conclusion

The audit identified formatting compliance issues in approximately 38% of reviewed documents. The issues are concentrated in a small number of recurring patterns that can be addressed through templates, training, and lightweight process controls. Implementing the four recommendations above is expected to raise the overall compliance rate to 90%+ by the end of Q3 2026.
